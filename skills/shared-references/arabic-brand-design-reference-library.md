# Arabic Brand Design Reference Library

Research snapshot: 15 November 2026. This is an additional set of 50 Arabic-market brand projects to complement `brand-design-reference-library.md`. Thirty-six projects center on Egypt, including one Egyptian-cuisine concept designed for a Greek market; the remaining fourteen cover Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Lebanon. Sources include public studio case studies, award books, and creator-published portfolio pages.

## How to use this library

- Treat the set as a source of design methods, not a moodboard to copy. Never reproduce a logo, custom lettering, recognizable illustration, exact palette, composition, packaging trade dress, or signature graphic.
- Most Behance pages are self-published. Where commission or launch status is not stated, the status is **unclear**. Entries explicitly described as concepts or unofficial redesigns are labelled. Do not claim business impact or real-world adoption without evidence.
- “Egyptian” refers to the stated brand market or Egyptian brief. A creator’s location alone does not prove the client or project is Egyptian; market/context notes flag this distinction.
- Notes are research cues from public descriptions and case framing. Re-open the source and inspect the work before making claims about exact colors, forms, materials, or execution.
- Use Arabic as a language and script with distinct audience, dialect, reading-order, and legibility requirements—not as a decorative texture. Ask the client which dialect/script hierarchy they need.

## Patterns across this Arabic-market set

- Treat bilingual composition as a content and reading-order problem. Decide which language leads, whether both scripts share weight, and what the shortest practical lockups are.
- Local references work best when tied to a real ingredient, founder story, ritual, service, craft, or place stated in the brief. Do not invent heritage, dialect, or symbolism from a country label.
- Build prompts around verified touchpoints. Hospitality, FMCG, fashion, and B2B work require different mockup families; ask the client to choose when the intended channel is unclear.
- For image prompts, preserve exact approved Arabic spelling and use the supplied logo as an image reference when supported. If a model cannot render Arabic accurately, generate an unbranded scene and add the real logo and copy later in an editable layout.
